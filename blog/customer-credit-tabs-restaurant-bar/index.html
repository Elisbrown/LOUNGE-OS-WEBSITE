Customer credit and tabs: how to sell on credit without losing money

Credit keeps your best customers coming back. It also quietly eats the cash you need for Monday's delivery. Here are the rules that keep tabs healthy, and how to replace the notebook.

By LoungeOS TeamPublished November 12, 2026Updated November 12, 20263 min read

Key takeaways

  • Decide who can have credit, how much and for how long before you give it, and write it down.
  • Only managers and cashiers should open a tab, and every tab sale should name the customer and the staff member.
  • Watch two numbers every day: total owed and the oldest debt.
  • A tab sale is a sale today; the payment is money in later. Mixing them up counts sales twice.

In many bars, lounges and neighbourhood shops, the credit notebook is the most valuable document in the house, and the least protected. Pages are torn out, amounts are argued over, and nobody knows the total until the owner sits down on Sunday with a calculator.

Why give credit at all?

Because it works. Regulars who can drink now and pay at the end of the month come more often, bring friends and spend more. Companies that send their staff for lunch want one bill a month. Refusing all credit can cost more than the debts.

The goal is not zero credit. It is credit you choose, can see and get paid for.

Five rules for healthy tabs

  1. A list of who can have credit. Regulars you know, with a phone number. Not “a friend of the boss”.
  2. A limit per customer. For example 50,000 FCFA. Above it, they pay something before they order again.
  3. A deadline. End of the month, or every Friday. Write it on the receipt.
  4. Who can open a tab. The manager or the cashier, not every waiter.
  5. A follow-up routine. Every Monday, call or message anyone owing for more than 30 days.

The two numbers to watch

  • Total owed. If it grows month after month while sales are flat, you are financing your customers.
  • Oldest debt. A debt older than 60 days is at risk. Older than 90, treat it as a likely loss in your planning.

Get the accounting right

A sale on credit is a sale on the day it happens, with no cash coming in. When the customer pays, that is money in, not a new sale. If your system counts both as sales, your figures are inflated and the cash never matches. In double-entry terms, the sale goes to receivables, and the payment clears the receivable.

Frequently asked questions

Should waiters be allowed to give credit?

It is safer to limit it to the cashier and the manager, so every credit decision has an owner.

What do I do with a customer who doesn’t pay?

Stop new credit first. Then agree on a payment plan, with part payments recorded as they come in.

Is a tab the same as a loyalty program?

No. A loyalty program rewards spending; a tab is money the customer owes you.
