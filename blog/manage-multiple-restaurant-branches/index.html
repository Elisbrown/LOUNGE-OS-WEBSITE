How to manage several restaurant or bar branches without losing control

The second branch is where many good businesses start leaking money. The owner can't be in two places, so the system has to be. Here is what to standardise, what to watch every day, and how to set it up.

By LoungeOS TeamPublished November 12, 2026Updated November 12, 20263 min read

Key takeaways

  • Standardise the menu, prices, recipes, payment methods and receipts before you open the second branch.
  • Keep stock and cash separate per branch, so a gap in one cannot hide in the other.
  • Watch five numbers per branch every day: net sales, average sale, cancellations and refunds, cash taken against cash counted, and stock alerts.
  • Give each branch a manager with approval rights and a log, and visit with the numbers, not instead of them.

Your first lounge works because you are there: you see who comes in, you count the crates, you notice when the cashier looks nervous. The second one runs on systems, or it runs on luck.

1. Standardise before you open

Decide what must be the same everywhere:

  • Menu and prices. Start from a copy of the first branch. Local price differences are fine, but decide them, don’t let them drift.
  • Portions and recipes. A 33cl gin and tonic in one branch and a 25cl one in the other is a margin problem.
  • Payment methods and references. Every branch records MoMo and OM with the transaction reference.
  • Receipts and documents with the same business name, tax number and footer.
  • Roles and rules: who can cancel, discount, refund, give credit.

2. Keep stock and cash separate

It is tempting to treat two branches as one big store room. Don’t. When stock moves between branches, record it as stock out in one and stock in in the other. Each branch counts its own stock and closes its own cash. Otherwise a gap in one disappears into the other and nobody is responsible.

3. Watch five numbers per branch, every day

  1. Net sales against the same day last week.
  2. Average sale. A sudden drop often means items are not being rung.
  3. Cancellations and refunds, with who approved them.
  4. Cash taken against cash counted at closing.
  5. Stock alerts: what ran out, what is low, and losses recorded.

Seen side by side, one branch’s unusual night stands out at once.

4. A manager per branch, with rights and a log

Each branch needs someone who can approve a cancellation at 23:00 without calling you. Give them the rights, and make sure every approval is recorded under their name. Review the log weekly together.

5. Visit with the numbers

Visits matter, but go in knowing yesterday’s figures. “Tuesday’s sales were 30% under last Tuesday and there were six cancellations after 22:00, what happened?” is a very different conversation from “how is it going?”.

Frequently asked questions

Should two branches share one stock?

No. Even if one supplies the other, record transfers as movements so each branch’s count stays true.

How many branches before I need a multi-branch system?

Two. The moment you can’t stand in both places, you need each branch’s numbers without being there.

Can the same staff work in two branches?

Yes, but add them to each branch’s team so their sales and approvals are recorded in the right place.
