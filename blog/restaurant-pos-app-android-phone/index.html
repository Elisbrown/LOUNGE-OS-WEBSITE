Running a restaurant or bar on Android phones: what works

Every waiter already has an Android phone in their pocket. With the right POS app, it becomes an order pad, the kitchen gets its tickets, and the owner sees the night's sales from home. Here is what works, and what still belongs on a till.

By LoungeOS TeamPublished November 12, 2026Updated November 12, 20263 min read

Key takeaways

  • Phones are excellent for taking orders at the table, kitchen and bar alerts and the owner's view of sales.
  • Keep a till computer at the counter for printing, the cash drawer and selling through internet cuts.
  • Good notifications go by role: the chef gets tickets, the waiter gets 'ready', the manager gets cancellations.
  • Give each person their own account, never a shared phone login.

A few years ago a restaurant POS meant a heavy terminal bolted to the counter. Today most of the work can happen on the phones your team already owns. The question is not whether phones can do it, but which jobs belong on a phone and which on a till.

What phones do well

  • Taking orders at the table. The waiter taps the table, adds items with photos and sends. No walking back to the counter, no handwriting for the kitchen to decode.
  • Alerts. The chef’s phone buzzes when a ticket arrives; the waiter’s when it is ready.
  • The owner’s view. Today’s sales, open bills and stock alerts, from anywhere.
  • Barcode scanning with the camera in a small shop, without a scanner.
  • Sharing reports as PDF or Excel on WhatsApp.

What still belongs on a till

  • Printing receipts and kitchen tickets to thermal printers, quickly and reliably.
  • The cash drawer, which opens from the receipt printer.
  • Selling through internet cuts. A till that keeps the business on its own computer keeps going when the internet does not.

The best setup for most venues: one Windows or Mac till at the counter, phones for waiters, the kitchen, the bar and the owner.

Notifications: by role, or not at all

A phone that buzzes for everything gets muted by the second night. Notifications should go to the person who must act:

Event Who needs it
Guest order from a table QR code Waiters and cashiers
New kitchen or bar ticket Chef or bartender
Order ready The waiter who took it
Order cancelled Managers
Stock low or out Stock manager and managers

And they should expire: an alert from two hours ago is noise.

Setup tips

  1. One account per person. Every order, cancellation and payment is then attributed to someone.
  2. Phones charged at the start of the shift, with a charging spot behind the bar.
  3. Good Wi-Fi in every area, separate from the guest network.
  4. Notifications allowed for the POS app, and tested before service.
  5. A home-screen widget for the owner and manager, so the day’s figures are one glance away.

Frequently asked questions

Can a small shop run on a phone only?

Yes, for selling and stock, if the internet is reliable. Add a till computer when you need receipts printed fast or selling through cuts.

Are staff phones safe for a POS?

With one account per person, a PIN or password, and the ability to deactivate someone who leaves, yes. Avoid shared logins.
